# Prompt: PRISM hackathon presentation

Copy everything below the line into an AI presentation tool (Gamma, Microsoft Copilot in PowerPoint, ChatGPT,

---

Create a 16-slide presentation for **PRISM**, our entry to the **DataQuest 3.0** hackathon, problem statement
**"Multi-Dimensional STEAM Career Guidance & Hyper-Local Innovation Platform"**. The audience is a judging panel
that will see it once. After the deck they must clearly understand: the problem, who suffers from it, what PRISM
does, how the scoring works, why it can be trusted, and that it is a working product, not a mock-up.

## Design rules

- **Theme:** dark. Background near-black `#07080D`, text off-white `#E8EAF2`, muted text `#8A90A6`.
- **Accent:** the six score colours in this order, used as a thin spectrum line and for the score parts, never as
  big blocks: violet `#7C6CFF` (Fit), blue `#3B8BFF` (Job market), teal `#14B8A6` (Affordability), green
  `#65C23A` (Return on investment), amber `#F2B01E` (Family alignment), red `#F0544F` (Automation risk).
- **Font:** one clean sans-serif (Inter, or Segoe UI / Calibri if unavailable). Big bold headlines, short lines.
- **Text:** at most 5 bullets per slide and 12 words per bullet. Prefer one strong sentence plus a screenshot.
- **Screenshots:** show them large (60–70 % of the slide) with rounded corners and a soft shadow. Never stretch or
  crop out the important part. Add a one-line caption under each.
- **Structure:** every slide title states the takeaway ("Parents see exactly where they disagree"), not a label
  ("Family module").
- Add slide numbers, plus a small "PRISM · DataQuest 3.0" footer.
- Write speaker notes for every slide: 2–4 sentences the presenter can say aloud.

### 2. The problem statement
- Title: "The brief: guidance that sees the whole student, and their home town"
- In our words, the problem statement asks for career guidance that:
  - looks at **many dimensions** of a student, not just marks
  - covers **STEAM** careers (science, technology, engineering, arts, maths)
  - connects students to **hyper-local innovation**: real problems in their own district
- Speaker notes: restate the brief in one breath, then say that slides 3–4 show why existing guidance fails at it.

### 3. Why this matters
- Title: "Most Class 10–12 students choose a career from three inputs"
- Three columns with icons: **Marks** · **What relatives say** · **What the neighbour's child did**
- Below them: "Aptitude tests stop at *'you suit engineering'*. They ignore the questions families actually argue
  about."
- Three questions in quotes, each in a different accent colour:
  - "Can we afford it, and will the loan be repaid?"
  - "Will there be jobs in four years, and near home?"
  - "Why does my child want design when we want medicine?"
- No screenshot. Keep it typographic and emotional.

### 4. Who it is for
- Title: "Four people, one decision"
- Four cards, one per role:
  - **Student:** discovers strengths and sees why each career fits
  - **Parent:** enters the budget and hopes privately, and sees where they and their child differ
  - **School counsellor:** supervises hundreds of students and sees who needs a conversation
  - **Admin:** monitors fairness, data freshness and outcomes
- Speaker notes: a school usually has one counsellor for a very large number of students. PRISM does the
  counselling at scale and saves human time for the cases that need it.

### 5. What PRISM does, end to end
- Title: "From a 25-minute questionnaire to a family plan"
- A horizontal six-step flow with arrows: **Questionnaire → 19-dimension profile → Six-part score → Money plan →
  Family meeting → 5-year roadmap**
- Screenshot: `02-how-it-works.jpg`

### 6. Step 1: understanding the student
- Title: "74 original questions measure 19 dimensions, not just marks"
- Bullets:
  - Interests (RIASEC), four aptitudes, thinking style, work values, grit, risk tolerance
  - Built-in quality checks catch rushed or contradictory answers
  - Can be taken in two sittings; progress is saved
- Screenshots: `04-questionnaire.jpg` (large) and `03-student-journey.jpg` (small, overlapping)

### 7. Step 2: the six-part score (the core idea)
- Title: "Every career gets one score, split into six visible parts"
- Show the formula large, colour-coding each term in its accent colour:
  `Score = 0.30 Fit + 0.15 Market + 0.20 Affordability + 0.15 ROI + 0.15 Family − 0.05 Automation risk`
- A small table: one line on what feeds each part (fit = 19-dimension profile match; market = salaries and live
  job postings by region; affordability = cost after scholarships against family income; ROI = 10-year earnings
  against starting work after Class 12; family = conflict index; automation risk = exposure to automation, the
  only part that subtracts)
- Screenshot: `05-results.jpg`
- Speaker notes: the landing page shows this as light through a prism splitting into six colours, which is where
  the name comes from.

### 8. Step 3: nothing is a black box
- Title: "Click any career to see exactly why it scored what it did"
- Bullets:
  - Each part's contribution in points
  - A likely score range, not a falsely precise number
  - Rank stability tested across 64 what-if scenarios
- Screenshots: `07-why-this-score.jpg` (main) and `06-results-ranking-stability.jpg` (inset)

### 9. Step 4: money made concrete
- Title: "The real cost, the scholarships, and the loan EMI, before the family commits"
- Bullets:
  - Fees per admission quota (government / management)
  - Scholarships the student actually qualifies for
  - Loan EMI checked against monthly income; warns when repayments strain the family
- Screenshots: `08-what-it-costs.jpg`, `14-loan-explainer.jpg`, `15-scholarships.jpg` as a staggered collage

### 10. Step 5: the family, built in
- Title: "Parents see exactly where they and their child disagree, and talk it through"
- Bullets:
  - The parent joins with an invite code and enters the budget privately
  - **Conflict index** across six areas: field, risk, distance from home, budget, time to earn, prestige vs
    stability
  - A guided **family meeting**: one question at a time, "We agree" or "Discuss later"
  - "Bridge careers" that satisfy both sides
- Screenshots: `09-family-conflict.jpg` (main), `11-family-meeting.jpg` and `10-family-budget.jpg` (smaller)
- Speaker notes: this is our most distinctive feature: PRISM treats the family disagreement as data, not
  as noise.

### 11. Step 6: explore and decide
- Title: "Change one thing and watch the list re-rank instantly"
- Bullets:
  - **What-if:** more savings, a different city, different priorities
  - **Compare:** two or three careers side by side
  - **Plan:** entrance exams, deadlines and free courses (SWAYAM, NPTEL) on a 5-year roadmap
- Screenshots: `12-what-if.jpg`, `13-compare.jpg`, `16-five-year-plan.jpg`

### 12. Hyper-local innovation (the second half of the brief)
- Title: "Real problems in the student's own district become starter projects"
- Bullets:
  - Enter a pincode and see problems from your district, with partner organisations
  - Each problem gives a 4–8 week **starter project** and the careers it leads to
  - Example: Dharmapuri students test village wells for fluoride and map the results
- Screenshot: `17-hyper-local-problems.jpg` (large)
- Speaker notes: this turns "STEAM careers" from an abstract list into something a rural student can start this
  month.

### 13. Trust, privacy and fairness
- Title: "Every number shows its source, and whether it has been checked"
- Two columns:
  - **Data honesty:** each figure is labelled *Checked* or *Estimate*; rankings are reproducible from stored data
    versions; the AI only rephrases summaries, never scores, and an answer with an invented number is rejected
  - **Privacy and fairness:** parental consent for under-18s; family money hidden from the student unless shared;
    no caste or category data ever collected; automated fairness checks
- Screenshots: `19-data-trust.jpg` and `20-parental-consent.jpg`
