The Problem

Career decisions are made in the dark — then paid for over decades.

Students, parents and educators navigate a system where demand signals, compensation trajectories and household affordability are invisible until commitments are already made.

◈

Geographic demand opacity

No visibility into which regions value a specialised skill right now, or where global demand is migrating next.

₹

Economic yield mismatch

Actual compensation trajectories and the ROI horizon after a course are guesswork, not modelled data.

⚖

Parental exclusion & blind spots

Household affordability is never systematically analysed — the primary decision-makers sit outside the model, producing debt and misaligned expectations.

✧

Unexplored career alternatives

Rigid conventional tracks crowd out emerging hyper-local and STEAM innovation pathways that are hiring today.

◷

Disconnected financial aid discovery

Entrance exams, institutional rankings and targeted scholarships are never timed against the decision window.

Systemic outcome
  • High career dropout rates
  • Unfulfilled aspirations
  • Graduate underemployment

The PRISM Engine deploys a comprehensive multi-vector algorithmic engine that balances student intrinsic traits, parental constraints and industry demand.

Core Concepts

The PRISM workflow — a structured multi-vector analysis pipeline

Every recommendation is traceable to a normalised vector, a solved constraint and a market index. No gut feel, no black box.

  1. 01

    Ingest multi-stakeholder inputs

    Psychometric assessment responses from the student; financial, aspirational and risk parameters from parents. Captured separately so conflicting incentives stay visible.

    aptitudecognitive preferenceinterest indicatorshousehold budgetrisk appetite
    S = [l, c, m, h, sci]P = [₹, σ, pri, min]
  2. 02

    Vectorize, normalise & solve constraints

    Min–max normalisation onto a common scale, the Financial Constraint Solver projects affordability over the full degree horizon, and the Parent–Student Conflict Index quantifies incentive divergence.

    min–max scalingconstraint solverconflict indexcomposite score
    z = (x − min)/(max − min)PCI = ‖ws − wp‖₁ / 2
  3. 03

    Synthesise market intelligence & generate roadmap

    Normalised vectors are blended with live hiring velocity, economic disruption indices and geographic demand, then emitted as an optimised, financially viable career roadmap with exams, pathways and scholarships.

    job velocitydisruption indexgeo-demandroadmapalignments gaps
    score = Σ wᵢ · vᵢroadmap = argmax(score)

How this is calculated

Composite = 0.36·fit + 0.24·financial + 0.14·risk + 0.26·market, minus a penalty when the starting yield falls below the family floor.

Parent–Student Conflict Index = ‖ws − wp‖₁ / 2 — how far apart the student's and the family's rankings sit, from 0 to 100. In practice it is computed from the Spearman rank correlation ρ as (1 − ρ)/2: ρ = 1 means the two orderings agree perfectly, ρ = −1 means they are exactly reversed.

Fit = cosine similarity between the student's trait vector and each career's ideal vector, nudged up when the declared interest matches the domain.
